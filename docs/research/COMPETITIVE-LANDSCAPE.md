# Competitive landscape: Swiss parliamentary intelligence and citizen companion

**Research date:** 2026-09-14  
**Scope:** public, currently observable Swiss products and official services relevant to (1) parliamentary video/transcript evidence, (2) federal popular-vote information, and (3) voluntary, non-binding participation. Local ballots are a later extension of the federal-first concept. Links below were accessed on 2026-09-14.

## Market truth

The public products reviewed divide the job across distinct surfaces. The Swiss Parliament publishes authoritative video, the Official Bulletin, Curia Vista procedure records, and chamber-vote data. VoteInfo publishes official federal, cantonal, and communal ballot/election information, reminders, videos, and results. easyvote/votenow explains ballots to younger voters, while smartvote matches voters with candidates and parties. Parlacta monitors parliamentary business, and Decidim, E-Mitwirkung, and Go Vocal provide consultation or participation workflows.

Across the public product pages reviewed, no one service exposes the complete chain of a citizen question to a source-linked parliamentary video moment with a documented second-level anchor, the federal ballot calendar/results, and a separate optional-identity non-binding consultation. That is an integration and trust-workflow gap, not proof that any individual component is technically unique. Existing products already cover parts of semantic search, mobile web/apps, reminders, AI analysis, anonymous/registered participation, and voting advice.

## What is observed versus advertised

- **Parlacta:** public search/filters/source-linked parliamentary monitoring are observable; AI summaries, AI assistant, reporting, and MCP are labelled planned. Pricing is explicitly a construction-stage planning page and not orderable.
- **smartvote:** voter matching and election profiles are observable; mobile-first improvements, personal archive, head-to-head comparison, and cross-election search appear on a crowdfunding roadmap. Treat roadmap language as proposed work.
- **Decidim:** responsive mobile-first web and API are documented; the older development documentation says a mobile app is expected, so native-app availability should be verified separately.
- **Go Vocal:** AI analysis, accessibility, offline input, and anonymous/registered modes are vendor-advertised capabilities. A Swiss deployment was not confirmed in the official case material reviewed.
- **Official Parliament:** official video, AI subtitles, and authoritative Bulletin text coexist, but the AI-subtitle disclaimer and the temporary current-session video outage show why evidence cards should retain original text, provenance, revision status, and uncertainty.

## Defensible whitespace hypotheses

1. **One evidence chain across debate, ballot, and source.** A citizen question could resolve to a stable evidence ID that opens the original parliamentary speech at a documented second, shows the checked Bulletin passage and translation, and links to the related federal ballot/business and date. Today, Parliament, VoteInfo, and Parlacta each cover different parts of that chain.

2. **Voluntary topic following for the federal-first citizen mode.** Let people choose topics and receive only relevant briefings, ballot dates, and source-backed video moments. VoteInfo already offers locality-based pushes/reminders, easyvote offers simplified ballot explanations, and Parlacta offers monitoring alerts; the proposed value is their source-linked combination with user-controlled topic scope.

3. **A visibly separate non-binding consultation with transparent identity tiers.** These are alternative policies to investigate, not interchangeable responses in one verified tally. The current product direction keeps reading open and gates the specified citizen consultation with an eligibility check; any unverified survey must have a separate cohort and result. Decidim, E-Mitwirkung, and Go Vocal already support variants of anonymous/registered participation, but no reviewed Swiss implementation exposed passport-derived eligibility for this use case. The product must never present this mode as the federal vote.

4. **Multilingual verification instead of personality or ideology scoring.** Use the Bulletin's original-language record, translation toggle, speaker/session metadata, source links, confidence/review state, and uncertainty. This addresses the gap between official but fragmented material and monitoring/advice products that classify or match positions without exposing a timestamped debate passage. It also respects the repository's rule against inferring ideology, emotion, intent, or sensitive traits.

5. **Reproducible personal/research evidence packs.** Stable IDs, exact time boundaries, original text, version/revision history, correction path, and exports could serve citizens, journalists, researchers, and staff. Existing services offer pieces such as source links, exports, privacy controls, or candidate matching, but no reviewed service combines those pieces across parliamentary video, ballot information, and consultation responses.

## Buyer and business-model hypotheses to test

- **Institutional buyer:** a parliament/cantonal administration, municipal participation office, or civic-education program could fund ingestion, multilingual source maintenance, hosting, moderation/review, and consultation administration. Citizens receive the core information experience free of charge.
- **Professional workspace:** newsrooms, NGOs, researchers, and public-affairs teams could pay for team alerts, saved evidence packs, exports, and revision notifications. Parlacta's planned CHF 129 monitoring tier and CHF 249 AI tier show a public pricing hypothesis, but they are not evidence of realized demand or live orders.
- **Mixed funding:** keep official ballot and source navigation open; charge institutions for workspaces, governance controls, analytics, and support. This resembles the disclosed patterns around smartvote (candidate/party/media services plus donations), easyvote (brochure/topic-clip sales plus public/private support), and consultation platforms (implementation/hosting/support), while avoiding ad targeting or party-sponsored rankings.
- **Procurement constraint:** identity/eligibility features should be a separately commissioned consultation module with data minimization and an explicit non-binding label. Interview buyers about whether they can legally and operationally sponsor such a credential flow before treating passport-derived eligibility as a requirement.

## Interview questions

**Citizens**

1. What would make you open the companion first: an upcoming federal ballot date, a topic you follow, a representative's speech, or a result update?
2. If an answer linked directly to the original speech at the exact second, how would that change your trust or willingness to share it?
3. Which language, translation, context, and uncertainty labels would you need before relying on an AI answer?
4. Which topics would you choose to follow, and which notifications would feel useful rather than noisy?
5. For a clearly labelled non-binding consultation, would you choose anonymous participation, an account, or optional eligibility proof? What would you fear about each option?
6. What wording would make it unmistakable that a consultation response does not cast or influence the official federal vote?

**Administrators, journalists, and researchers**

7. Who would own corrections to ballot dates, transcripts, translations, and video boundaries, and what review evidence would be required?
8. Which export fields are essential: stable evidence ID, original passage, translation, speaker, session/business number, timestamps, source URL, confidence, and revision history?
9. Would optional passport-derived eligibility improve consultation legitimacy enough to justify procurement, integration, support, and privacy work? Which credential or data should never be retained?
10. What duplicate-response, anonymous-abuse, and moderation risk is acceptable for voluntary consultation?
11. Which budget would pay: parliament/canton/municipality, civic education, newsroom/NGO, or a shared public service? What outcome would justify renewal?
